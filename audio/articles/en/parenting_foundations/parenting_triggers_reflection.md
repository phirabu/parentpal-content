# Your Parenting Triggers and Patterns

Take a moment to honestly examine your own patterns and triggers as a parent. This reflection helps you understand your automatic responses and create space for more intentional parenting.

## Part 1: Identifying Your Triggers

Think about the last few times you felt frustrated, angry, or overwhelmed with your child. Write down:
- What behavior triggered your reaction?
- What emotions did you experience?
- What did you say or do in response?
- How did your child respond to your reaction?

## Part 2: Exploring Your History

Consider your own childhood experiences:
- How did your parents handle conflict or strong emotions?
- What messages did you receive about expressing feelings?
- Which of your current responses mirror what you experienced as a child?
- What do you want to do differently?

## Part 3: Your Emotional State

Reflect on how your current life circumstances affect your parenting:
- Are you getting adequate sleep, nutrition, and self-care?
- What external stressors (work, relationships, health) impact your patience?
- How do these factors show up in your interactions with your child?

## Part 4: Creating Awareness

Moving forward, practice noticing:
- Your physical sensations when you start feeling triggered (tight chest, clenched jaw, etc.)
- The thoughts that precede your reactions ("They're doing this on purpose," "They should know better")
- The gap between feeling triggered and responding—this is where choice lives

## Questions for Deeper Reflection

- What kind of parent do you want to be when your child looks back on their childhood?
- What would change if you truly believed your child is doing their best with their current developmental capacity?
- How might your relationship with your child change if you prioritized connection over compliance?

Remember: Awareness is the first step toward change. Be compassionate with yourself as you grow.
